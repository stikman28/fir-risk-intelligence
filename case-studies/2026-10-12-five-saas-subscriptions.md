Five logins. Five renewal notices a year. Five sets of numbers that never quite matched.

That's where a specialty retailer I work with started this year: NetSuite for accounting, Rithum for the marketplaces, NetStock for inventory planning, OZ WMS in the warehouse, and a SaaS point-of-sale system in the store. Every contract renewed higher. Each system connected back to NetSuite through its own integration, some solid and some fragile, so the same data was copied from system to system. And the company didn't really control any of it.

So we built one platform instead, on a data foundation the company owns.

We ran it side by side with NetSuite for three monthly closes before anyone relied on it. It went live in mid-May. Today the books update as sales happen, month-end closes in one day, and the team uses Claude every day on their own numbers.

The part I'm proudest of: the company's developer took on the warehouse system himself, designed it, built it, and was promoted to Director of Operations once it was live.

All five subscriptions are gone.

The lesson I'd give any CEO: own your data. Everything else, AI included, depends on it.

## SOURCE DATA

- Five SaaS replaced (Bruce, 10/10): NetSuite (accounting), Rithum (marketplace channel mgmt), NetStock (inventory planning), OZ WMS (warehouse), SaaS POS. All integrated via NetSuite (Rithum also to marketplaces; POS integration poor but worked).
- Timeline: data foundation ~2 months; NetSuite history migrated; 3 parallel closes + 4-month tie-out; go-live mid-May 2026 (never publish the exact date); 4 months of tuning/UI/adoption; September automation; one-day close.
- 150+ updates since go-live (169 transaction-service versions after go-live per version history).
- Client and developer are never named. Developer promoted to Director of Operations after go-live.
