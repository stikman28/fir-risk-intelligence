# FIR Risk INTEL-52 — What SEC Cyber Filings Don't Tell You

**Type:** `FILING INTEL`
**Date:** November 7, 2026
**Platform Source:** FIR Risk E96 — The Costliest 5%: What Confirmed Losses Teach Us About Planning (Cyentia Institute, IRIS Xtreme 2026)

---

## The INTEL

**SEC 8-K cyber filings are a weak guide to how large cyber losses get. Of 205 incidents reported on 8-Ks since the 2023 SEC rule, the filing companies assessed only 6% as material or likely material, and only 22 included any estimate of financial impact, according to the Cyentia Institute's analysis of filings tracked by Board Cybersecurity.**

Cyentia's IRIS Xtreme 2026 analyzed the 205 incidents in aggregate:

- **Materiality:** 7 assessed as material and 5 as likely material by the companies themselves; the other 94% were not material or still under evaluation
- **Loss figures:** 22 of 205 gave an estimate; Cyentia calls that sample "more of a curiosity than a basis for credible model parameters"
- **Reputation:** appears as a "possibly material" impact in 2 of the 205
- **Operations:** 91 of the 205 (44%) describe at least one operational impact, most often disruption to business operations and system outages

Cyentia also found "little overlap between 'material' and 'extreme' events": the incidents filed as material are mostly a lower tier than the costliest events in the study.

---

## Why It Matters

Public disclosures are an easy input for a risk assessment, and the 8-K rule made them more visible. But Cyentia describes the 8-K record so far as "as much a record of regulatory uncertainty as it is a record of genuine material risk," reflecting cautious early filing under a new rule and estimates that were often not updated.

That doesn't make the filings useless. They are a good record of how incidents hit operations: outages, order fulfillment, production. What they don't provide yet is a reliable basis for sizing losses. Loss data from confirmed events does that better.

The reputation finding is worth a second look too. Reputational damage often drives where cyber scenarios sit on a risk register, yet companies flagged it as possibly material in 2 of 205 filings.

---

## What To Do — One Key Action

**Use public disclosures for what they show well, operational impact, and size losses from outcome data such as loss studies by revenue band. If a scenario on your risk register is ranked mainly on reputational impact, check what evidence supports that ranking.**
