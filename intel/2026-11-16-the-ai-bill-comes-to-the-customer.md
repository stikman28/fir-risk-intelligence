# FIR Risk INTEL-55 — The AI Bill Comes to the Customer

**Type:** `TREND`
**Date:** November 16, 2026
**Platform Source:** FIR Risk E97 — The Capacity Race: Five Signals From Big Tech's Risk Disclosures (SEC filings from Microsoft and Oracle)

---

## The INTEL

**The companies building AI capacity are telling investors their costs may not come down, while AI prices may. That gap will be closed by their customers. Expect your cloud and enterprise software costs to rise, even as AI gets cheaper per task. Your only real leverage is a renewal you are prepared to walk away from.**

What the filings say, in the companies' own words:

- **Costs may not fall.** Microsoft added that if AI costs "increase, remain elevated, or fail to decline, or if pricing for AI products and services declines as a result of competition, commoditization, or other market forces," its margins could suffer. It also says it may have to pay "prices above prevailing market rates" to secure scarce components.
- **Fixed prices are now a risk to the supplier.** Oracle added that rising energy costs "can adversely affect our margins, particularly where customer pricing is fixed or committed." When a supplier lists your fixed price as one of its risks, expect it to try to change that price at renewal.
- **Long leases, short customer contracts.** Oracle's cloud contracts "have durations of one to five years; are renewed at the customer's option," while its data-center leases "do not align with the duration and pricing of customer contracts." If customers don't renew, it says it could be "locked into multi-year commitments for excess data center space... without receiving corresponding revenue."

---

## Why It Matters

**Our read:** the builders with the deepest customer bases, Microsoft, Amazon, Alphabet and Oracle, have committed hundreds of billions of dollars to capacity. That spending will be recovered from the customers who already depend on them: through cloud rates, committed-use contracts, and the price of the software subscriptions they already pay for. The price of a single AI task can fall while the total bill rises.

Oracle's filing also shows where the leverage sits. The customer's option not to renew is a risk Oracle discloses to its own investors. That leverage only exists if the customer is willing to use it. A customer with no costed alternative, no migration plan and no time left before renewal will pay what the supplier asks, and the supplier knows it.

This is not a reason to avoid AI or cloud. It is a reason to budget for rising technology costs instead of assuming AI will make them cheaper, and to prepare for renewals early.

---

## What To Do — One Key Action

**Before your next major cloud or enterprise software renewal, cost a real alternative for the critical workloads involved and decide in advance which terms you would walk away from. Start early enough that walking away is possible. Without a credible exit, you have no leverage.**
