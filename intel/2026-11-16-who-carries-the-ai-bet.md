# FIR Risk INTEL-55 — Who Carries the AI Bet Depends on the Business Model

**Type:** `TREND`
**Date:** November 16, 2026
**Platform Source:** FIR Risk E97 — The Capacity Race: Five Signals From Big Tech's Risk Disclosures (SEC filings from Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft and Oracle; Anthropic, PBC Form S-1/A)

---

## The INTEL

**Every major AI builder is committing hundreds of billions of dollars to computing capacity, but their latest SEC filings show the risk of that bet lands very differently depending on how each company makes its money. Most organizations sit in the weakest position: they rent.**

The business models, from the filings:

- **Builders that also sell the capacity: Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet.** They build for their own products and rent capacity to everyone else, so cloud customers help pay for the bet. Microsoft's Azure revenue was $33.2 billion in its September quarter, up from $22.4 billion; AWS sales grew 39%.
- **A seller that leases what it sells: Oracle.** It leases much of its data-center capacity long term to serve cloud contracts that run one to five years and renew at the customer's option. Its new risk factors say those leases "do not align with the duration and pricing of customer contracts."
- **A builder for its own use: Meta.** It generates "substantially all" of its revenue from advertising and has no cloud business to resell capacity, while reporting $454.52 billion of non-cancelable commitments.
- **A renter: Apple.** Its capital spending fell to $9.0 billion from $12.7 billion. It relies on outside cloud providers for AI computing and says there are "limited third-party providers that can reliably meet such demand."
- **A model maker: Anthropic.** It buys capacity from the builders and sells AI to businesses, 44% of it through those same builders' cloud marketplaces.

---

## Why It Matters

Companies that resell capacity can pass part of the cost of the race to their customers. Companies that build for their own use carry it themselves. Companies that rent depend on the builders' prices and priorities, and Apple, one of the world's largest technology companies, now discloses that dependence as a risk.

Most organizations are renters. The business model of each critical provider shapes three things that matter to them: how it will price at renewal, what it will prioritize when capacity is short, and how exposed it is if AI demand arrives more slowly than it is betting.

This is not about picking winners. It is about knowing which kind of supplier you depend on before you sign a multi-year commitment.

---

## What To Do — One Key Action

**For each critical AI and cloud provider, record which business model it runs in your third-party review, and use it to stress-test your next multi-year commitment: what happens to your costs and capacity if that provider raises prices, rations capacity or changes terms?**
