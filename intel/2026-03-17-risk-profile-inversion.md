## The INTEL

For a decade, the #1 risk on every CISO's board presentation was the same: **ransomware locks our systems, operations stop, we pay to recover.** Business interruption. The loud, visible, urgent threat.

That risk has been surpassed.

Four consecutive 2026 annual threat reports — from Unit 42, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike, and now Picus — converge on the same conclusion. The data from the Picus Red Report makes it undeniable:

**Old Risk:** Business interruption from ransomware
**New Risk:** Undetected long-term access and silent data exfiltration

Ransomware encryption dropped **38% in one year.** But credential theft is now at **23% prevalence — double the rate of encryption.** Eight of the top 10 attack techniques are designed for stealth, not destruction. Adversaries have shifted from smash-and-grab to what Picus calls the "Digital Parasite" — inhabiting your environment for months, feeding on your identity systems, extracting value continuously, and remaining invisible.

**The adversary who's been inside your network for six months — mapping your data flows, harvesting credentials, understanding your business processes — is a larger financial and operational risk than the one who locks your files and demands Bitcoin.**

---

## Why It Matters

Most enterprise risk registers, board reports, and security investment strategies are still built around the ransomware disruption model. Incident response plans optimize for containment and recovery. Tabletop exercises simulate encryption events. Insurance policies cover business interruption.

But the threat that now appears twice as often as encryption — silent credential theft followed by long-term access — doesn't trigger any of those controls. There's no alert. No business interruption. No ransom note. The adversary is inside, and your security program is designed to detect a threat that's becoming less common while the dominant threat is designed specifically to evade everything you've built.

---

## What To Do

**Update your risk register to reflect the inversion.** The primary threat scenario is no longer "ransomware encrypts our systems" — it's "an adversary has had undetected access to our environment for 6+ months." If your top-rated risk is still ransomware disruption, your risk register doesn't match the 2026 threat landscape. Adjust severity, likelihood, and your detection strategy accordingly. The adversary you can't see is now more dangerous than the one making demands.
